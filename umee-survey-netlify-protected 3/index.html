Section 1

Overall survey summary

Each wave is a separate LimeSurvey export from u‑mee's annual (now more frequent) customer survey. Before analysing anything, each file needed cleaning — and the cleaning itself is part of the story.

2024

Fielded from 6 June 2024

1,423 valid respondents (of 4,288 raw rows — see data-quality note below). No eero, Amazon-ecosystem, GT Fibre or household-composition questions were asked this year.

2025

Fielded from 24 July 2025

2,233 valid respondents, one row per token, no duplication issues. First wave to cover the eero Wi-Fi rollout, Amazon devices, GT Fibre competition and household age composition.

2026

Fielded from 17 Aug 2026, data as of 8 Sep 2026

1,414 valid respondents so far. Adds a new question on Nokia router/ONT ownership. Fielding window is shorter than prior years to date, so treat the final 2026 total as provisional.

Data-quality note: the 2024 file needed real cleaning

Data rule applied The 2024 export contained 4,288 rows but only 2,093 unique respondent tokens — 1,238 tokens appeared more than once (one token appeared 24 times), almost certainly a rapid-resubmission or autosave artefact in the survey tool rather than 24 genuine responses. The workbook's own second sheet (“Sheet2”) lists 1,423 tokens as the validated respondent set — a subset of the 2,093 — which we take to be u‑mee's own definition of a completed, eligible response (e.g. for the prize draw). All 2024 figures in this report use that 1,423-respondent base, taking the most recent submission for any token with more than one row in the valid set. No answers were invented or estimated to fill gaps; where a question wasn't asked in a given year, or a respondent skipped it, that is shown as — or a smaller sample size, not a filled-in value.

Missing / incomplete data by question

Every rating and yes/no question in this report shows its own sample size (n) alongside the result, because completion rates vary a lot by question — core questions (Overall happy, u‑mee ratings, Recommend) were answered by effectively 100% of valid respondents in every year, while some downstream or skip-logic questions (eero app features, TV brand comparisons, Amazon device ownership) were answered by anywhere from 40% to 95% of respondents, either because they only apply to a subset (e.g. eero brand questions only ask non-eero households) or because respondents skipped optional items. Where a sample size drops below 100, we flag it explicitly rather than presenting the percentage as if it were as reliable as a full-sample figure.

Section 3

Key trends

Five patterns stand out across the three waves — not as isolated numbers, but as movements that hold up consistently year after year.

1. A steady, structural drift from broadcast to streaming

The share of customers watching “only u‑mee” content has fallen every year (16.4% → 12.9% → 10.4%), while “mostly/only streaming” has risen every year (20.6% → 24.8% → 29.2%). This isn't a single-year blip — it moves the same direction in both year-on-year steps, and it's mirrored by rising Prime Video and Apple TV adoption. Two-thirds of customers still expect their balance to stay “the same” over the next five years, but among those expecting change, more expect to shift toward streaming than back toward broadcast, in every year.

2. eero has solved the Wi-Fi hardware problem, fast

In one survey cycle, eero adoption reached ~90% and stayed there. The clearest consequence: the share of customers relying on their own separately-bought Wi-Fi kit collapsed from 27.5% (2024, pre-rollout) to 8.8% (2026). Before/after comparisons from eero adopters are strongly positive (58% report better reliability, only 2.5% report worse), and this effect is consistent across both years it's been tracked — a genuine, durable product win rather than a one-off launch bump.

3. Two well-built features are quietly losing visibility

Awareness of eero Secure (a free bundled security subscription) fell from 44.5% to 37.0% in a single year, and awareness of Smart DNS fell from 66.8% (2024) to 59.4% (2026). Both are differentiating, no-extra-cost features that customers increasingly don't know they have — the opposite of what you'd expect as a rollout matures and communication should compound.

4. u‑mee TV's edge over competitors is narrowing — slightly, but consistently

All six sub-dimensions of the "rate u‑mee TV against other providers" question (channels, reliability, picture quality, programme guide, catch-up, personal recordings) moved down between 2024 and 2026, by between 0.08 and 0.17 of a point. No single dimension moved on its own — they all drifted the same way, which is a stronger signal than any one of them individually. u‑mee TV is still rated highly in absolute terms (4.3–4.4/5 throughout), but the competitive gap appears to be closing.

5. Underlying satisfaction is remarkably stable — and it's service quality carrying it, not content breadth

Overall happiness and recommendation intent have both dipped only slightly (happy: 98.3% → 97.2%; recommend: 98.0% → 96.7%) despite the streaming migration and softening TV-comparison scores. The five core service ratings (Features, Reliability, Quality, Support, Value) have stayed essentially flat across all three years — within 0.05 of a point on every dimension. That combination suggests customers are increasingly getting some of their content elsewhere, while still rating the underlying u‑mee service itself just as highly as before.

Section 4

Biggest insights

Patterns and relationships in the data that go beyond the year-on-year headline numbers.

Value for money is the strongest driver of detractorsCustomers who would recommend u‑mee rate “Value” at 4.03/5 on average; those who wouldn't recommend rate it 2.20/5 — the single widest gap of any of the five core ratings (see Section 6). Reliability and Quality show similarly wide gaps. This points to perceived value, not any one specific feature, as the sharpest edge between promoters and detractors.
Streaming migration is concentrated in family householdsHouseholds with children under 18 are far more streaming-oriented than those without: 36.5% are "mostly/only streaming" vs. 24.0% of childless households, and "mostly u‑mee" viewing is more than twice as common in households without children (33.5% vs. 14.5%). Kids' content interest is six times higher in households with children (37.7% vs. 6.5%, as expected) — but the wider streaming-balance gap suggests family households are quietly leading the shift away from linear TV.
A small, curious signal among eero non-adoptersThe ~9% of 2026 respondents who have not installed eero actually rate their home Wi-Fi reliability marginally higher (4.22/5) than eero adopters (4.14/5), yet report lower overall happiness (95.2% vs. 97.4%). A plausible reading: many non-adopters already had good Wi-Fi and saw no reason to switch, while their slightly lower happiness reflects other frictions (e.g. an unreturned spare unit, or general resistance to a mandated hardware change) rather than the Wi-Fi itself. Sample size is modest (n=124), so treat this as a lead for further investigation rather than a settled finding.
Two "hidden gem" features risk staying hiddeneero Secure and Smart DNS both offer genuine, no-extra-cost value — and both have falling awareness (see Trend 3). Because they cost nothing extra to use, closing the awareness gap is a low-cost way to lift perceived value, directly addressing the "Value" driver identified above.
No contradictions between related answers — a good sign for data qualityDirectionally-related questions move together as expected throughout: WiFi-vs-before comparisons are positive exactly where eero adoption is highest; streaming-app adoption rises in step with the "mostly/only streaming" viewing-balance answers; and households reporting more devices connected via Ethernet also report higher device counts generally. This internal consistency increases confidence in the other findings in this report.

Section 7

Open-text responses

None of the three surveys includes a general "additional comments" free-text question. The open-text material available consists of "Other, please specify" fields attached to multiple-choice questions — useful for spotting themes, but limited in volume. The most substantive of these are summarised below.

Why some customers keep their spare eero instead of returning it (2026, n=11)

A small group of respondents who received a spare eero and haven't returned it explained why. Two clear themes:

  • Keeping it as a future backup or expansion unit (8 of 11 responses) — e.g. wanting it available "in case I need it eventually," or to cover an additional room later.
  • Uncertainty about the return process itself (a smaller number) — one respondent wasn't sure they were supposed to return it at all, suggesting the return instructions may not be fully clear to everyone.

What attracts customers to streaming, beyond the standard options (2026, n=13)

Free-text responses to "what features attract you to streaming" beyond the standard list clustered around three themes:

  • Content not available on u‑mee — sport, K-dramas, and Spanish-language content were each mentioned specifically.
  • Simplicity and ease of use —ted directly against friction with u‑mee's own platform. One respondent specifically cited not knowing about u‑mee's Smart DNS or recording capability, tying back to the awareness gap identified in Section 3.
  • Frustration with specific u‑mee TV UX details — one respondent called out an "irritating voice-over" on the catch-up interface as a specific annoyance.

"Simplicity. No complexity at all; I didn't know about u‑mee's DNS server, about the recording capability, but even if I did, I am not sure I would use it if it's complex or time consuming to use."

Why some customers still use their own router/AP instead of eero (2026, n=10)

A handful of respondents explained their own Wi-Fi setup in free text. Reasons given include: existing high-end personal equipment already in place (a central Cisco access point, a bridged router), a desire for greater range than eero alone provides in larger properties, and difficulty getting eero installed with adequate support. This is a small sample, but it echoes the pattern in Section 5: some non-adopters have equipment they're already satisfied with, rather than being unaware of or resistant to eero.

No new complaint or praise theme in the free-text fields appears to be genuinely new to 2026 versus 2025 — the volume of open text is too low in both years to support a reliable "emerging theme" comparison. We'd recommend adding a proper open-ended comments question to future surveys (see Section 11) specifically to enable this kind of analysis.

Sample sizes to treat with caution (n < 150)

  • Eero non-adopters, 2026 (n=124): the segment comparison in Section 5 is directionally useful but should not be treated as precise.
  • "Intend to install eero" among non-installers, 2026 (n=124) and 2025 (n=233): percentages here can swing several points with only a handful of respondents changing answer.
  • Returned spare eero, 2025 (n=91) and 2026 (n=57): both are small sub-populations (only people who received two eero units and haven't installed both) — treat the 12.1% / ~12% "returned" figures as broadly consistent rather than precisely comparable.
  • Own-brand router figures (e.g. TP-Link, D-Link, HP, Cisco) in 2025/2026: several brands have single-digit or low-double-digit response counts in these years, because the eligible population (non-eero users) shrank to roughly 100–230 people. Percentage-point comparisons against 2024 (n=1,423) should be read as "this brand has become rare," not as a precise trend line.
  • GT Fibre / Gibtelecom copper ownership (n=17–51 depending on year and service): genuinely small minority segments; stable in proportion but based on modest counts.

Distribution shape: ratings are skewed positive, not evenly spread

Across every 1–5 rating question in this survey (u‑mee ratings, Wi-Fi ratings, TV-vs-competitor ratings), the distribution is consistently skewed toward 4s and 5s, with a long thin tail down to 1. For example, the 2026 "Value" rating (mean 4.10) has 67% of respondents choosing 4 or 5, and only 5.4% choosing 1 or 2. This is typical for a satisfied customer base and means the mean is a reasonable, non-misleading summary statistic throughout this report — medians for every core rating sit within 0.2 of the reported mean in all three years, so we've reported means only, for readability.

What looks like meaningful movement vs. normal year-to-year noise

With sample sizes in the thousands, a 1–2 percentage-point shift in a binary Yes/No metric (like Overall happy) is within the range of ordinary sampling variation and shouldn't be over-read on its own. What we've treated as meaningful in this report are patterns that (a) move consistently in the same direction across both year-on-year steps rather than reversing, and/or (b) show up simultaneously across multiple related questions (e.g. the six TV-vs-competitor sub-scores all moving down together, or eero-app awareness and eero-Secure awareness both declining together). Single-metric, single-year movements of a point or two — like the small dip in Netflix usage — are noted but flagged as directional rather than conclusive.

Section 10

Where things stand today (2026)

A closer look at the most recent wave on its own terms.

What's improved

  • Ease of streaming setup is at its highest point yet (4.16/5, up from 3.87 in 2024) — the largest positive rating movement in the whole dataset.
  • eero adoption has risen further, to 91.2% of respondents, with continued strongly positive before/after Wi-Fi comparisons.
  • Reliance on self-purchased Wi-Fi kit is now down to 8.8% of the customer base, from 27.5% before the eero rollout.
  • Amazon Prime subscription among customers has reached 54%, its highest recorded level.

What's worsened

  • Awareness of eero Secure and Smart DNS — two free, differentiating features — both continue to decline.
  • u‑mee TV's rating against competitors has softened across all six comparison dimensions versus 2024, though it remains solidly rated in absolute terms (4.3–4.4/5).
  • "Only u‑mee" viewing has fallen to its lowest recorded share (10.4%).

Biggest current opportunity

Close the awareness gap on eero Secure and Smart DNS. Both are already-built, no-extra-cost features; the barrier is communication, not product. Given that "Value" is the sharpest driver of recommendation gaps (Section 6), improving awareness of free included features is a low-cost way to lift perceived value.

Biggest current concern

The slow, consistent softening of u‑mee TV's comparative standing against streaming competitors, combined with the steady structural drift toward streaming (especially in family households), suggests the content/TV platform side of the business — not connectivity — is where competitive pressure is building.

Based only on evidence within the three survey datasets.

Immediate priorities

  • Run a short, targeted awareness push for eero Secure and Smart DNS. Both are free, already-included features with falling awareness across two consecutive waves. A simple in-app notification, email, or bill-insert explaining "you already have this, here's how to turn it on" could move the needle quickly and cheaply.
  • Clarify the spare-eero return process. Open-text responses show at least one customer unsure whether they were supposed to return an unused unit; tightening this messaging could reduce confusion without needing new infrastructure.

Medium-term opportunities

  • Investigate the TV-vs-competitors softening with a focused content/feature benchmarking review. Because the decline is small but consistent across all six sub-dimensions, it likely reflects a genuine, gradual competitive shift rather than noise — worth understanding before it compounds further.
  • Consider a family-focused content or bundling strategy. Households with children are streaming-migrating fastest; understanding what specifically draws them away from u‑mee TV (kids' content library breadth, in particular) could inform investment priorities.
  • Follow up qualitatively with the small group of eero non-adopters to understand whether their choice reflects satisfaction with existing kit, installation friction, or something else — the survey data hints at both but can't fully separate them at n=124.

Areas to monitor in next year's survey

  • Whether the streaming-migration trend continues at the same steady pace, accelerates, or plateaus.
  • Whether eero Secure / Smart DNS awareness responds to any communication effort taken.
  • Whether the TV-vs-competitors softening continues across all six dimensions or stabilises.
  • Value-for-money ratings specifically, given their outsized relationship with recommendation intent.

Questions to add to future surveys

  • A genuine open-ended "anything else you'd like to tell us?" question. The current surveys have almost no free-text comment capacity outside of "Other, please specify" fields tied to specific multiple-choice questions — this limits open-text analysis considerably (Section 7).
  • A consistent respondent ID or household ID across years, to allow true panel (same-household-over-time) analysis rather than only cross-sectional year-on-year comparison.
  • Keep the "what brand is your own Wi-Fi kit" question, but ask it of everyone (not only non-eero households), so brand-share trends remain comparable over time as the eligible population shrinks.
  • A direct price/value perception question (e.g. "how does u‑mee's price compare to alternatives"), given how strongly Value correlates with recommendation intent.
Section 12

Executive summary

The story of the last three years is stability at the core, with two things quietly moving underneath it. u‑mee's fundamental service quality — reliability, features, quality, support — has stayed essentially flat and very high across all three survey waves (all ratings within 0.05 of their 2024 level), and overall satisfaction remains excellent (97.2% happy, 96.7% recommend in 2026, down only marginally from 98%+ in 2024).

What's improved: the eero Wi-Fi rollout has been a clear, durable success — adoption reached ~90% within its first tracked year and has kept climbing, self-purchased Wi-Fi kit has become the exception rather than the norm (27.5% → 8.8% of customers), and streaming setup is rated meaningfully easier than it was three years ago.

What's declined: awareness of two genuinely valuable, no-extra-cost features — eero Secure and Smart DNS — is falling even as adoption of the underlying products rises. And u‑mee TV's rating against streaming competitors has softened by a small but consistent margin across every dimension measured, alongside a steady, structural drift of viewing time toward streaming apps, concentrated especially in households with children.

What's changed most significantly: the elimination of the self-purchased Wi-Fi kit market is the single largest, cleanest shift in the dataset. The steady erosion of feature awareness (eero Secure, Smart DNS) is the most actionable, since both are already-built, zero-cost fixes.

What respondents are telling us: connectivity and hardware are no longer where the risk sits — content competitiveness and communicating included value are. Value-for-money shows the widest rating gap between promoters and detractors of any dimension measured, more than reliability or features.

What to pay most attention to next: whether the TV-vs-competitors softening and the streaming migration continue at the same gradual pace or accelerate — and whether a low-cost awareness campaign on eero Secure and Smart DNS can measurably shift the Value perception gap identified in the cross-question analysis.

Analysis based on 5,070 valid survey responses across the 2024, 2025 and 2026 u‑mee customer surveys.
